The United States FDA approves very few clinical indications for beta-carotene as a formal, labeled medical treatment.

Because beta-carotene is naturally classified and regulated under the Dietary Supplement Health and Health Education Act (DSHEA), conventional and integrative physicians rely on specific therapeutic pathways for prescription or monitored clinical deployment.

The primary FDA-approved clinical uses, along with major evidence-based protocols managed by integrative clinicians, are outlined below:

I. Official FDA-Approved Medical Indications
--------------------------------------------

1.  Erythropoietic Protoporphyria (EPP) Amelioration

    -   The Indication: This is the primary standalone, FDA-approved therapeutic indication for high-dose beta-carotene.
    -   The Treatment Protocol: Clinicians prescribe massive daily doses (typically 30 mg to 300 mg per day for adults) to provide systemic, light-filtering skin protection. The molecule physically accumulates inside the dermal layers to act as a systemic shield against ultraviolet and visible light radiation, preventing severe phototoxic burning and skin cell lysis in patients suffering from this rare genetic metabolic disorder.

2.  FDA-Regulated Infant Formula Nutrient Addition

    -   The Indication: Under [21 CFR §184.1254](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/Beta%20Carotene%20Synthetic%20Supplemental%20TR.pdf), the FDA legally approves and mandates the strict use of beta-carotene as a highly controlled, vital source of safe provitamin A in commercial infant formulas. It protects infants against accidental hypervitaminosis A toxicity because the infant body will only break it down based on immediate metabolic need.

II. Non-FDA Approved But Standardized Clinical Protocols
--------------------------------------------------------

While not formally "FDA-approved indications" for pharmaceutical labels, conventional, functional, and integrative medical protocols standardize beta-carotene for the following evidence-based treatment strategies:

-   Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AREDS Protocol): Under the National Eye Institute's rigorous clinical guidance, physicians utilize beta-carotene (15 mg daily) explicitly paired with vitamin C, vitamin E, copper, and zinc. This specialized formulation reduces the progressive risk of advanced macular degeneration and vision loss in high-risk patients.
-   Polymorphous Light Eruption (PMLE): Dermatologists and integrative physicians utilize controlled high-dose cycles (75 mg to 180 mg daily) during spring and summer months to systematically reduce systemic photosensitivity flare-ups and light-induced hives in sun-sensitive individuals.
-   Malabsorption-Induced Vitamin A Deficiency: Gastroenterologists select natural beta-carotene to slowly rebuild vitamin A pools in patients suffering from structural pancreatic insufficiency, short bowel syndrome, or genetic malabsorption disorders.

III. Critical In-Clinic Treatment Warnings
------------------------------------------

-   The Smoking/Asbestos Contraindication: Due to definitive data from the [ATBC Cancer Prevention Study](https://atbcstudy.cancer.gov/), physicians explicitly screen out all current or former smokers, as isolated high-dose carotene shifts from a protective antioxidant into a highly toxic pro-oxidant inside smoke-damaged lung tissue.
-   The Alcohol/Liver Warning: Functional and integrative physicians closely monitor liver panels, as concurrent high alcohol intake combined with high-dose carotenoid therapy accelerates localized hepatotoxicity and structural liver damage.
