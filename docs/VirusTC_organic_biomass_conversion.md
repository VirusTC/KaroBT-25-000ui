In physical chemistry, when electromagnetic energy transfers from an incoming wave (frequency) into a substance like beta-carotene, it is calculated using the quantum parameters of that specific medium.
Beta-carotene ($C_{40}H_{56}$) features an extensive system of 11 conjugated carbon-carbon double bonds. This long, alternating structure acts as a specific electronic medium that restricts what frequencies it can absorb.
The step-by-step mathematical conversion of energy transfers within a beta-carotene medium is detailed below:

1\. The Absorption Profile of the Medium
----------------------------------------

Beta-carotene absorbs light waves within the blue spectrum. According to standard data parameters from [PhotochemCAD](https://www.photochemcad.com/databases/common-compounds/polyenes-polyynes/beta-carotene), its maximum peak absorption wavelength ($\lambda_{\text{max}}$) in a non-polar solvent baseline is precisely 451 nanometers (nm).
-   Wavelength ($\lambda$): $451 \times 10^{-9} \text{ meters}$
-   Speed of Light ($c$): $\approx 3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$

* * * * *

2\. Step 1: Converting Medium Wavelength to Frequency
-----------------------------------------------------

To find the exact resonant frequency ($\nu$) matching this optimal absorption band, we apply the wave equation:

$$\nu = \frac{c}{\lambda}$$

$$\nu = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}}{451 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}}$$

$$\mathbf{\nu \approx 6.65 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz } (665 \text{ THz})}$$

* * * * *

3\. Step 2: Calculating Energy Transfer Per Photon
--------------------------------------------------

Next, we calculate the energy ($E$) carried by an incoming wave at this frequency using the Planck-Einstein relation:

$$E = h\nu$$

-   Planck's Constant ($h$): $6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$

$$E = (6.626 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}) \times (6.65 \times 10^{14} \text{ s}^{-1})$$

$$\mathbf{E \approx 4.41 \times 10^{-19} \text{ Joules per photon}}$$

To convert this value into electronvolts (eV) for molecular orbit scales, divide by the elementary charge constant ($1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J/eV}$):

$$E_{\text{eV}} = \frac{4.41 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J/eV}} \approx \mathbf{2.75 \text{ eV}}$$

* * * * *

4\. Step 3: Macroscopic Energy Scaling Per Mole
-----------------------------------------------

In chemical solutions, energy transfer is measured across macroscopic volumes using [Avogadro's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avogadro_constant) ($N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ particles/mol}$) to determine the total energy transfer per mole of the medium:

$$E_{\text{total}} = E \times N_A$$

$$E_{\text{total}} = (4.41 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J/photon}) \times (6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ photons/mol})$$

$$E_{\text{total}} \approx 265,570 \text{ J/mol} \approx \mathbf{265.6 \text{ kJ/mol}}$$

5\. Molecular Result of Energy Transfer
---------------------------------------

When beta-carotene absorbs this specific frequency energy ($265.6 \text{ kJ/mol}$), the energy transfers into its $\pi$-electron system.

-   The Transition: It drives an electronic shift from the ground state ($S_0$) to the second excited singlet state ($S_2$).
-   The Dissipation: Because beta-carotene cannot store this energy, it rapidly releases the energy within $\approx 100 \text{ femtoseconds}$ through non-radiative decay, converting the wave energy entirely into minor localized heat vibrations throughout its carbon chain.
